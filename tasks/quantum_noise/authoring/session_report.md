## Selected task 1: spectral correlation audit

### Why the target is meaningful and hard

The participant must move from randomized raw count rows to a SPAM-robust
observed-error distribution and then use that distribution to test a disclosed
local graphical model and rank nonlocal residual interactions. The target leaf is
not the task boundary: the backward slice has seven operations and four linked
scientific/output rubric branches.

The hidden spectral generator is constrained to physical observed-error vectors
under the paper's Pauli-support visibility map. This is a private generator/oracle
invariant, not paper trivia participants must infer.

Independent challenge sources include nonlinear multi-mode fitting with nuisance
amplitudes and finite counts, transform/bit/simplex conventions, dependence and
graphical-model calculations, and hidden shifts in labels, length grids, shot
totals, SPAM, and correlation structure.

### Included workflow subgraph

1. XOR-correct and aggregate repeated counts.
2. Normalize outcome histograms and compute the disclosed Walsh transform.
3. Fit nuisance-amplitude decays over irregular lengths.
4. Invert the spectrum and construct a valid simplex distribution.
5. Compute marginal, MI, CMI, covariance, and Pearson diagnostics.
6. Reconstruct the disclosed local clique-tree approximation and natural-log JSD.
7. Rank nonlocal interactions missed by the local model.

### Participant versus hidden information

Participant-visible design:

- neutral task statement and all scientific conventions;
- a manifest, randomized target histograms, and local clique-tree specification;
- explicit CLI and schemas;
- required analyzer plus `aggregated.csv`, `spectra.csv`, `decays.csv`,
  `distribution.csv`, `dependence.csv`, and `summary.json`;
- qualitative metric directions, resource budget, and offline environment.

Hidden by design:

- generated cases and seeds;
- latent channel, SPAM, correlation, and anomaly parameters;
- exact references, thresholds, metric weights, ordinary/OOD/adversarial split;
- evaluator implementation details and mutant suite;
- paper, official repositories, and reported device answers.

## Selected task 2: scalable local noise model

### Why the target is meaningful and hard

The participant receives independently sampled, statistically inconsistent local
count tables on a disclosed running-intersection clique tree. They must reconcile
overlaps, build a normalized model, answer arbitrary partial-assignment queries
without enumerating all `2^n` states, and audit held-out parity interactions. This
is a grounded extension of bounded-local noise reconstruction, not the paper's
open topology-learning problem.

The paper's supplement states bounded-degree polynomial GRF learning only up to
an overall normalizing factor and delegates the global-consistency/error-bound
proof to Ref. 28. The benchmark's bounded junction-tree width, explicit
normalization, arbitrary-query semantics, and validation audit are therefore
grounded benchmark specializations, not claims that the paper directly proves.
The hidden `q*` distributions in this task are generic classical binary models;
the package does not claim they all arise from physical Pauli-channel observed
error distributions.

Independent challenge sources include numerical reconciliation under public
smoothing/weighting semantics, exact bounded-width inference, ordered scope/table
conventions, held-out predictive anomaly ranking, and hidden topology/label/width/
sample-size shifts.

### Included workflow subgraph

1. Parse ordered scopes, LSB-first tables, queries, and validation records.
2. Smooth and reconcile noisy overlapping marginals.
3. Construct normalized root and conditional junction-tree factors.
4. Answer arbitrary partial-assignment queries by bounded-width inference.
5. Predict held-out parity interaction counts.
6. Rank interactions missed by the local model.
7. Report normalization, separator, inference, and audit diagnostics.

### Participant versus hidden information

Participant-visible design:

- `manifest.json`, `clique_counts.json`, `queries.jsonl`, and `validation.jsonl`;
- exact binary/table/query/parity conventions and deterministic CLI;
- `model.json`, `query_results.jsonl`, `audit.json`, and `diagnostics.json` schemas;
- behavioral success dimensions and bounded-width resource expectations.

Hidden by design:

- generated ordinary, OOD, scale, and injected-anomaly cases;
- latent exact factor models, query answers, anomaly labels/strengths, and seeds;
- exact thresholds, weights, grader sandbox, references, and mutants;
- source paper, code, and device-specific content.

## Tolerance status

The planned policy separates structural tolerances from scientific quality:

- schema, finiteness, probability range, and normalization gates use only small
  serialization/numerical allowances;
- scientific reconstruction, held-out prediction, query, dependence, and ranking
  quality receive continuous scores over multiple hidden instances;
- final absolute/relative tolerances and score anchors must be calibrated from
  repeated oracle, clean-room, and alternative-solver runs, then stress-tested
  against realistic mutants;
- exact source values, coefficients, code bytes, weights, and plot pixels are not
  correctness targets.

For the verified checkout-stable local build, float64 invariant errors were at most `4.44e-16`. The private
contract therefore allows `3e-10 + 3e-8*scale` for query/model consistency,
`8e-9 + 3e-7*scale` for audit numerics, and `5e-9 + 2e-7*scale` for diagnostics,
over 100 times the observed independent arithmetic disagreement. Statistical
quality is scored with calibrated continuous bands rather than exact estimator
equality: mean-Hellinger anchors 0.045/0.160 and held-out log-RMSE anchors
0.055/0.260 with a `1e-14` probability floor. Both valid solvers pass, while all
eleven realistic mutants failed hard gates or mandatory/per-case category floors
in that independently reproduced final build.

For the verified checkout-stable spectral build, deterministic comparisons use `max(abs_tol,
rel_tol*abs(reference))`: aggregation/WHT `2e-10`/`2e-9`, fits
`2e-9`/`2e-7`, inverse/simplex `5e-9`/`5e-7`, and dependence/local/summary
`5e-8`/`5e-6`. Independent implementations agree within those bands; the
truth-oracle maximum consistency error is `4.440892098500626e-15`, and the
latent visibility inverse round-trip error is `3.3306690738754696e-16`. Finite-shot
scientific anchors are distribution TV 0.025 and eigenvalue RMSE 0.010. These
allow numerical/estimator freedom while all fourteen realistic mutants fail.
